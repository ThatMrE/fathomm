Why Kobe, Why Now/ 02

Kobe is not a backdrop — it is the accelerant.

The city pairs the deepest regenerative-medicine cluster in Asia with the fastest path from bench to approved product anywhere on earth. iPS-cell technology was born here; the world's first transplant of iPS-derived tissue was performed here in 2014; and in March 2026 Japan approved its first two iPS-cell therapies on a single day.

  • TerrainThe Kobe Biomedical Innovation Cluster — 334 members, ~12,700 staff, 8 hospitals and 1,500 beds on one island.
  • RegulationConditional, time-limited approval lets cell therapies reach market on early-stage data — years ahead of the FDA / EMA.
  • NetworkRIKEN BDR, FBRI's PIC/S-GMP cell-manufacturing centre, and the lineage of the world's first iPS-cell transplant.
  • CapitalAMED non-dilutive grants, a registered-VC co-invest network, and Kobe operating costs roughly half of Tokyo.

The Regulatory Edge/ 03

Approved on early data — the conditional pathway.

Japan's 2014 dual laws — the PMD Act and the Act on the Safety of Regenerative Medicine — created a distinct legal class for regenerative products and a uniquely fast route to market. We coach for it honestly: toward real efficacy, not just early approval.

1

Conditional approval

Demonstrate safety and probable benefit from small, early-stage trials — even single-arm, surrogate-endpoint data.

2

Market & reimburse

Sell and earn reimbursement during a confirmatory window of up to 7 years — a commercial bridge unavailable under the FDA / EMA.

3

Confirm or withdraw

A second PMDA review confirms efficacy for full approval, or the product is withdrawn — patient access with a built-in check.

SAKIGAKE + orphan fast-tracks target a ~6–9 month PMDA review, versus ~12 standard. Sources: PMDA · MHLW · Cell Stem Cell.

Who Should Apply/ 05

Bring a real, testable asset — and a reason to be in Japan.

Because residents actually use the lab and the weeks are short, the profile that succeeds is specific. We back cell therapy, gene therapy, and medical-device companies — split into a Biotech track and a Medtech track — at the concept-or-prototype stage, ideally already past first prototyping.

  • A validated concept or working prototype — a device, assay, or cell/gene construct that is past first prototyping.
  • At least one hands-on technical team member on the ground who can run wet-lab or device work (1–3 people, full duration).
  • A clear Japan rationale and a view on the regulatory route you want — PMDA, conditional/SRM, or Sakigake.
  • A defined experimental plan: which experiments, what materials, and no ethics-committee approval required for this pilot.
  • Ecosystem fit — rare disease, oncology, diagnostics, autologous cell therapy, or medical devices.
  • A collaborative, coachable posture — the program runs on peer learning, office hours, and Demo Day.
