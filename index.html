The demand

A market measured in millions.

Medical tourism is no longer a fringe choice — it's a mature, fast-growing global market, expanding at double-digit rates year after year.

Rising home-country costs, long public waitlists, and world-class specialization abroad have turned a trickle into a tide. The numbers are no longer speculative.

Why the demand exists

Four forces push people to the water.

Patients rarely travel for one reason alone — cost, access, expertise, and time compound until leaving home becomes the rational choice.

💰

Cost

Procedures run 40–90% cheaper abroad. A hip replacement near $50,000 in the U.S. can be under $10,000 in India; dental work saves up to 85%.

⏳

Wait times

Where public systems back up for months or years, an accredited clinic abroad can schedule care in weeks — a decisive factor for many electives.

🔑

Access

Treatments that are restricted, backlogged, or simply unavailable at home — from routine electives to the experimental frontier.

🏅

Expertise

Some destinations lead the world by sheer volume: Turkey for hair, Korea for facial contouring, India for cardiac and orthopedic surgery.

The problem worth solving

Demand outruns trust.

The flight is the easy part. The hard part is knowing whether the clinic is any good, what it truly costs, and what happens if something goes wrong.

  • ✦
    No standard way to compare. Accreditations, outcomes, and prices sit behind marketing pages and middlemen — patients can't line clinics up side by side.
  • ✦
    Opaque, shifting pricing. Advertised prices rarely match the itemized, all-in bill. Hidden markups thrive in the gap.
  • ✦
    Real downside risk. Ghost surgery, hair mills, and unproven "frontier" therapies punish the uninformed. Trust is the scarce resource.
